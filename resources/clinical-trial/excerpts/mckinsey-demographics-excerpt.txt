EXECUTIVE SUMMARY: KEY FINDINGS
================================================================================

OVERVIEW
--------
Our analysis evaluated 50 major U.S. academic medical centers and NCI-designated
cancer centers across six dimensions:
  1. Patient Volume (cancer-specific annual case load)
  2. Demographic Diversity (racial/ethnic representation, age distribution)
  3. Geographic Accessibility (catchment area, drive time analysis)
  4. Trial Infrastructure (research coordinators, beds, imaging capacity)
  5. Socioeconomic Mix (insurance coverage, income levels)
  6. Disease Specialization (oncology sub-specialties, tumor boards)

Each site received a composite "Trial Suitability Score" (0-100 scale) based
on weighted criteria optimized for Phase III oncology trials.

TOP INSIGHTS
------------
• The top 10 sites account for 38% of all Phase III oncology trial enrollment
  in the U.S. despite representing only 12% of cancer centers
• Sites in the Northeast and Texas have 2.3x higher patient density than
  national average for triple-negative breast cancer specifically
• Diversity scores vary dramatically: best sites have 45%+ racial/ethnic
  minority representation vs. worst at <10%
• Catchment area is critical: sites with >5M population within 90-min drive
  enroll 60% faster than those with <2M population access

NEXT STEPS & SITE SELECTION FRAMEWORK
================================================================================

HOW TO USE THIS DATA:
---------------------
1. IDENTIFY YOUR PRIORITIES
   • Is patient volume your #1 constraint? → Focus on sites ranked 1-5
   • Do you need specific demographic diversity? → Check Diversity Index
   • Is geographic coverage important? → Balance Northeast, South, West Coast
   • Do you have budget for multiple sites? → Select 3-5 from top 10

2. VALIDATE AGAINST YOUR INCLUSION CRITERIA
   • Match disease subtype (e.g., TNBC) to site-specific volumes
   • Ensure sufficient Stage II/III patients if that's your target
   • Check age distribution matches your study design

3. ASSESS INFRASTRUCTURE NEEDS
   • Complex trials (frequent imaging, biopsies) → Sites with robust facilities
   • Multi-cohort trials → Sites with large coordinator staff
   • Fast enrollment timelines → Sites with proven rapid activation

4. BALANCE RISK
   • Don't over-concentrate in one region (regulatory diversity risk)
   • Mix high-volume sites with fast-enrollment sites
   • Consider backup sites if budget allows

METHODOLOGY NOTES:
------------------
• Patient volume data: Aggregated from hospital discharge records (HCUP
  database), state cancer registries, and SEER incidence rates
• Demographic data: Linked ZIP code-level census data to patient records
• Infrastructure: Site surveys + publicly available institutional data
• Trial performance: Site-reported metrics + ClinicalTrials.gov outcomes

LIMITATIONS:
• Data reflects 2019-2023 period; recent changes may not be captured
• TNBC-specific volumes estimated using national subtype prevalence rates
  (22-24%) applied to site-level breast cancer diagnoses
• Insurance mix represents payer at admission, not longitudinal coverage
